About Mittiful Things

Mittiful Things is a handicraft house based in Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, founded by Ramwati Dhakad. It exists for one reason: this city has embroiderers, potters and brass workers with real skill, and very little organised demand for what they make.

Our approach is deliberately the reverse of most retail. Instead of designing a product and then sourcing the cheapest possible production, we start from the crafts already practised here and build the product around them. The maker decides what the work is worth. We build the price outward from that figure rather than squeezing it downward.

The result is a narrow range, produced slowly, in small batches. We would rather sell fewer things well than many things thinly.

Ramwati Dhakad

What we hold to

Six things we have decided not to trade away, even when it would be cheaper.

  • हाथ सेEvery stitch goes in by hand. One sleeve is about two days of one woman's work — we have never once run it through a machine and called it handmade.
  • पूरा दामThe maker is paid the day she hands the piece over, at a rate she named. If it then sits unsold for six months, that is our loss to carry, not hers.
  • यहीं काEverything is made within about ten kilometres of the fort. If it cannot be made here, we do not sell it.
  • थोड़ा-थोड़ाTwenty or thirty pieces at a time. When a batch is gone it is gone until the next one, and we would rather tell you that than keep a godown full of stock.
  • एक जैसा नहींYours will not match the photograph exactly. The peacock's tail curls differently every time. That is the proof it was made, not printed.
  • सीधी बातAsk who made yours and we will tell you her name, what it is made of, and honestly how long it will take.

Where it is made

Gwalior sits under a fort built on a single long block of sandstone, with the Chambal ravines an hour to the north. Almost everything on our things comes from somewhere within sight of it.

बत्तखThe ducks on our mark are not an invention. They are tiled in blue and yellow across the front of Man Singh Palace, and have been sitting there for roughly five hundred years.
बीहड़The winding line stitched across the sleeves is the Chambal beehad — the ravine country north of the city, drawn the way it looks from above.
पीतलThe meander border we chase into brass is cut into doorframes all over the old city. Our metalworker learned it from his father, off a doorway.
